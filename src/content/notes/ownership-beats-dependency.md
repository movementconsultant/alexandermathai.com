Every fast-moving brand or business ends up with a stack of rented dependencies: the page builder, the link-in-bio tool, the platform-native storefront, the third-party form service, the social account that is, functionally, the entire customer relationship. None of that is wrong at the start. Renting is often the correct call when speed matters more than durability.

The problem shows up later, quietly. The rented tool becomes load-bearing. Pricing changes, a platform policy shifts, an account gets suspended for no clear reason, or the vendor sunsets the product — and what was supposed to be a convenience turns out to have been the foundation.

Ownership doesn't mean building everything from scratch or refusing every platform. It means knowing, deliberately, which parts of a system are core enough that renting them is a real risk — and owning those: your domain, your content model, your customer data, your ability to move the thing if you have to. Everything else can stay rented as long as it's genuinely replaceable.

That's the standard applied to this site, and to how the rest of the ecosystem gets built: static, portable, content you can export, no dependency that would strand the business if it disappeared tomorrow.
